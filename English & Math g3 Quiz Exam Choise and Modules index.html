Mathematics • Topic 1

Multiplication & Division (2-Digit by 1-Digit)

Key Rules to Remember:
  • Written Multiplication: Multiply the ones digit first, then the tens digit. Regroup (carry) whenever a product is 10 or more.
  • Division with or without Remainders: Divide starting from the tens digit on the left. Any leftover amount smaller than the divisor is written as R (Remainder).
  • Checking Division: (Quotient × Divisor) + Remainder = Dividend
  • Two-Step Word Problems: Solve Step 1 to find the hidden helper number, then use that answer in Step 2.
Example A: Written Multiplication
Find 28 × 4:
1. Ones: 8 × 4 = 32 (write 2, carry 3) 2. Tens: (2 × 4) + 3 = 11 tens Answer = 112
Example B: Division with Remainder
Find 74 ÷ 3:
1. Tens: 7 ÷ 3 = 2 remainder 1 2. Bring down 4 → 14 ÷ 3 = 4 R 2 Answer = 24 R 2 (24 × 3 + 2 = 74)
Example C: Two-Step Word Problem
3 boxes hold 24 cupcakes each. 19 are sold. How many are left?
Step 1: 24 × 3 = 72 cupcakes total Step 2: 72 − 19 = 53 cupcakes left
Mathematics • Topic 2

Money (Dollars and Cents)

Key Rules for Money:
  • $1.00 = 100 cents. Always line up the decimal dots vertically when adding or subtracting.
  • Write two digits after the decimal point for cents (for example, $4.05, not $4.5).
  • To find change, subtract the total cost from the amount paid.
Example D: Adding Money
$14.35 + $8.25
$14.35 + $8.25 -------- $22.60
Example E: Subtracting & Change
Buy items for $11.25. Pay with $20.00:
$20.00 − $11.25 -------- $8.75 change

English • Grammar Rules

Grammar Review & Sentence Patterns

1. Linking Verbs (taste, sound, smell, look, feel)

Linking verbs connect a subject to an adjective describing how it seems to our senses. Add -s for singular subjects in the present tense:

  • The lemon tastes sour.
  • Those drums sound loud.
  • The silk scarf feels smooth.
2. Past Tense: was & were

Use was for one person/thing; use were for more than one (and with you):

  • The bear was inside the cave.
  • We were at the rain forest yesterday.
3. Cause & Effect: why & because
  • Why asks for a reason: "Why is the ground muddy?"
  • Because gives the reason: "It is muddy because it rained."
4. to-Infinitive for Purpose (to + base verb)

Explains the goal or purpose of an action:

  • Bees fly to flowers to collect nectar.
  • We wear thick coats to stay warm in the snow.
